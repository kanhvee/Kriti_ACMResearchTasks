## Paper Title

How to Read a Paper

## Author

S. Keshav

## Summary

How to Read a Paper
The paper explains that researchers spend a lot of time reading research papers, but how to read a paper efficiently is rarely taught. To avoid wasting time, Keshav introduces the Three-Pass Approach, where each pass has a different purpose instead of reading the paper from beginning to end in one go.
Pass 1 :- Bird’s-Eye View: This is a quick 5–10 minute scan. Read the title, abstract and introduction, then section and subsection headings, conclusions and references. The goal is to understand the basic idea and answer the Five Cs: Category, Context, Correctness, Contributions and Clarity. Based on this, you can decide whether the paper is worth reading further.
Pass 2 :- Understand the Content: Now read more carefully, but ignore detailed proofs. Focus especially on figures, diagrams and graphs, checking whether axes are labelled correctly and whether results have proper evidence such as error bars. Mark useful references that you have not read. After this pass, you should be able to explain the paper’s main idea and supporting evidence to someone else. This usually takes up to one hour.
Pass 3 :- Deep Understanding: This is mainly for papers that need detailed understanding, such as when reviewing them. The key idea is to virtually re-implement the paper—recreate its work using the authors’ assumptions. Challenge every assumption, compare your approach with theirs and identify innovations, hidden assumptions, missing citations, strengths, weaknesses and possible future work. It can take about 4–5 hours for beginners.
Literature Survey:- Start with 3–5 recent papers, study their related work and identify repeated citations and authors. Then find key researchers, their recent publications and important conferences. Finally, examine recent conference proceedings and keep adding important papers until the survey becomes complete.
Overall, the method helps control how deeply and how much time you spend reading each paper.
